# Ambit Journey — Made in China

_Five things people made. Five places with extraordinary stories._

**Status:** Approved for development; registration deferred  
**Format:** 5 rounds · 15 possible points  
**Registration:** Deferred

## Premise

Discover China through the things its people have created: regional cuisines, porcelain, monumental defenses, architecture, and modern cities.

The Journey moves from the intimate to the immense, and from centuries-old craftsmanship to transformation within living memory. Geography matters, but it serves the story rather than becoming the subject of every question.

### Opening

China has given the world countless things to taste, admire, and wonder at. Across five stops, we'll follow the people and places behind some of its most remarkable creations.

---

## 1. A Country of Kitchens

**Mechanic:** `choice-match`  
**Theme:** Food and regional culture

### Lead-in

First, something familiar: food. But there is no single Chinese cuisine. Across this enormous country, regional kitchens have developed flavors and traditions all their own.

### Prompt

Match these three dishes to the cities that made them famous.

### Interaction

Present three food photographs and match each dish to its associated city.

| Dish               | City      |
| ------------------ | --------- |
| Mapo tofu          | Chengdu   |
| Dim sum            | Guangzhou |
| Biangbiang noodles | Xi'an     |

The globe is centered on China. Correct associations are revealed geographically after submission.

### Hint

One city is famous for Sichuan's fiery cooking. Another is a center of Cantonese cuisine, while the third is known for the hearty wheat noodles of Shaanxi.

### Assist

Chengdu is matched to mapo tofu. Complete the remaining two associations.

### Reveal

From Sichuan's famously fiery cooking to the delicate traditions of Cantonese dim sum, geography has helped shape what ends up on the table.

Three cities, three very different traditions. And China's influence traveled much farther than its kitchens.

**Visual payoff:** Chengdu, Guangzhou, and Xi'an illuminate on the globe, each associated with its dish.

## 2. Blue and White

**Mechanic:** `city-single`  
**Theme:** Art, craftsmanship, and international trade

### Lead-in

For centuries, another Chinese creation found its way onto tables around the world. Its delicate blue patterns became so desirable that an entire type of fine ceramic still carries the country's name: _china_.

### Prompt

Find Jingdezhen, the city known as China's porcelain capital.

### Interaction

Show an elegant blue-and-white porcelain vessel alongside four anonymous city markers in eastern China.

Players select the city they believe is Jingdezhen.

**Answer:** Jingdezhen, Jiangxi province.

### Hint

Look inland in southeastern China. The city became a manufacturing center even though it wasn't a major coastal port.

### Assist

Jiangxi province is highlighted and labeled. The player must still select the correct city marker.

### Reveal

In Jingdezhen, generations of craftspeople transformed clay, minerals, and fire into porcelain prized around the world. Its workshops supplied imperial courts and international markets alike.

Some Chinese creations were made to travel. Others were built to control who could cross a frontier.

**Visual payoff:** Jingdezhen appears on the globe, accompanied by porcelain and historic kiln imagery.

---

## 3. The Wall That Wasn't a Border

**Mechanic:** `map-multiple`  
**Theme:** Geography, history, and architecture

### Lead-in

The Great Wall is one of the world's most recognizable structures. But here's the surprising part: it doesn't simply follow China's modern border.

And despite its name, it isn't one uninterrupted wall.

### Prompt

Which three of these six Chinese provinces contain historic sections of the Great Wall?

### Interaction

Six labeled provinces are available for selection directly on the globe.

**Correct selections:**

- Gansu
- Hebei
- Shanxi

**Distractors:**

- Sichuan
- Jiangsu
- Zhejiang

Players select three provinces and submit.

The six candidates are presented as a deliberately limited set. The question does not imply that only three provinces in China contain Great Wall remains.

### Hint

Think north. Different dynasties built defenses across China's northern landscapes, from the dry northwest toward the mountains nearer the coast.

### Assist

Gansu is selected and locked. A photograph of the western Jiayuguan fortifications offers additional geographic context.

### Reveal

The Great Wall is better understood as a vast network of fortifications, constructed and rebuilt by different dynasties over many centuries.

Some sections cross dry plains; others climb mountain ridges. Their locations tell stories about historic frontiers and changing military priorities—not the boundaries of today's China.

The wall was built to protect empires. Elsewhere, China's rulers, merchants, and architects built monuments to wealth, power, and ambition.

**Visual payoff:** The correct provinces illuminate, then the globe reveals the broader northern distribution of Great Wall remains, including sections beyond the three selected provinces.

## 4. Four Frames of History

**Mechanic:** `choice-order`  
**Theme:** History, architecture, and visual chronology

### Lead-in

Across China's long history, people have left remarkable structures behind. Some were made for emperors. Others reflected international commerce or a new vision of the future.

Four photographs tell that story across more than two thousand years.

### Prompt

Arrange these four Chinese landmarks from oldest to newest.

### Interaction

Present four recognizable photographs as reorderable cards, without dates.

**Correct chronological order:**

1. **Terracotta Army — near Xi'an**, late third century BCE
2. **Forbidden City — Beijing**, completed 1420
3. **Shanghai Customs House — the Bund**, completed 1927
4. **Beijing National Stadium — Bird's Nest**, completed 2008

Use the Customs House as the specific photographed Bund landmark so that the date is unambiguous.

### Hint

Two of these places belong to imperial China. The other two reflect the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.

### Assist

The Terracotta Army is placed and locked in the earliest position. Arrange the remaining three.

### Reveal

An army of clay soldiers was buried for China's first emperor. More than sixteen centuries later, the Forbidden City became the seat of imperial power in Beijing.

Shanghai's waterfront grew into a showcase of international commerce, while the Bird's Nest stadium became an architectural centerpiece of the 2008 Olympics.

Four creations, four radically different moments in China's history.

And the country's transformation didn't stop with its most famous monuments. Farther south, another city was changing almost beyond recognition.

**Visual payoff:** The four locations illuminate in chronological sequence, with dates appearing beside their photographs.

## 5. A City Reinvented

**Mechanic:** `pin` / `pin-distance`  
**Theme:** Modern China, manufacturing, and urban transformation

### Lead-in

Our final stop takes us to a city transformed within living memory.

In 1980, Shenzhen became one of China's first special economic zones. Over the following decades, manufacturing, investment, and migration helped transform it into a major metropolis.

### Prompt

Hong Kong is marked. Place a pin near Shenzhen, the city that grew just across the boundary.

### Interaction

Show an archival photograph from Shenzhen's early reform period alongside its contemporary skyline.

- Hong Kong is marked as a geographic reference.
- The player places a pin on the globe.
- Distance from Shenzhen determines the result.
- Use Ambit's established three accuracy bands, calibrated for the local map scale.

### Hint

Look directly north of Hong Kong, on the eastern side of the Pearl River estuary.

### Assist

Guangzhou appears as a second reference point. Shenzhen lies much closer to Hong Kong.

### Reveal

Shenzhen's factories helped connect China to global manufacturing networks. Its technology companies, dense neighborhoods, and skyscrapers tell another story: how rapidly a city—and an economy—can change.

From ancient kilns to modern megacities, China's creations have repeatedly reshaped the places where people live.

**Visual payoff:** Shenzhen is revealed, followed by a comparison of its earlier urban landscape and contemporary skyline. Preserve the player's submitted pin.

## Completion — Five Ways to Make a World

The globe revisits the five discoveries, preserving the player's actual answers and highlighting each stop's distinctive contribution:

- Three regional cuisines and the cities behind them.
- Jingdezhen and its world-famous porcelain.
- The Great Wall and the historic frontiers it crossed.
- Four landmarks spanning centuries of architecture and ambition.
- Shenzhen and its extraordinary urban transformation.

### Closing caption

_A meal. A porcelain vessel. A wall. A monument. A metropolis._

Across centuries, the people of China have shaped how the world eats, trades, builds, and lives. These five creations tell only a fraction of that story—but each began somewhere.

### Decisions and constraints

**Locked creative decisions:** Five-stop order, subjects, core question interactions, narrative transitions, and completion concept.

**Implementation flexibility:** Camera coordinates, IDs, exact candidate-city locations, presentation adapters, image layout, and numerical grading thresholds follow current repository conventions.

**Known extension:** Round 4 reuses an existing interaction but requires a compatible chronology-grading contract for landmark cards. Do not replace the approved ordering puzzle with multiple choice solely to avoid this adaptation.

**Geographic integrity:** Round 3 explicitly uses three correct provinces from a six-province candidate set. On reveal, distinguish the selected examples from the complete distribution of historic Great Wall remains.

**Verification:** Review the precise geographic and historical evidence, image licenses and credits, accessibility, hint/assist behavior, and visible wrong-answer feedback. Apply Ambit's existing acceptance gates and focused verification practices.

**Publication:** Do not register or deploy without authorization.
