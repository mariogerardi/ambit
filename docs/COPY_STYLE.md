# Player-facing copy

Use this guide for shared interfaces and authored content. For Journey structure,
clues, fairness, and reveals, also follow [Journey crafting](JOURNEY_CRAFTING.md).

## Voice and presentation

Write with curiosity, warmth, and concrete geographic detail. Be evocative before
the challenge, precise during it, and insightful after the answer. Preserve
intentional storytelling and puzzle-title punctuation rather than shortening all
copy into quiz labels.

- Use U.S. English, except for proper names, historical quotations, and official
  source terminology. Use **U.S.** in ordinary prose and labels.
- Write **Ambit** in prose and metadata; lowercase **ambit** may remain in the
  wordmark. Use **Today’s Journey**, **Journey Archive**, **Minigames**, **Explore**,
  and **Study** when naming those sections.
- Use sentence case for buttons, settings, labels, and utility headings; preserve
  proper nouns and established Journey titles. Omit terminal periods on action
  labels. Preserve intentional punctuation in editorial puzzle titles.
- Prefer natural contractions and typographic apostrophes in authored prose.
  Preserve official proper-name spellings, quoted source wording, and accepted
  typed-answer aliases. Never normalize identifiers or geographic datasets as a
  prose cleanup.
- Use en dashes for ranges, consistent units such as **km**, and localized number
  formatting. Check singular/plural agreement in every dynamic branch.

## Actions and instructions

Use **Select** for countries, territories, and map features; **Place a pin** for
coordinates; **Draw** for drawing mechanics; and **Type**, **Enter**, and **Submit**
for text entry and answer confirmation. Mention clicking, tapping, or dragging
when a specific gesture needs explanation.

A prompt must identify the required action. Prefer an imperative for interactive
map tasks. Lead-ins establish context without repeating the task. Button labels
must describe what actually happens: saving an image and copying a caption are
different actions.

Explain what to find, how to answer, and when a minigame ends in **How to play**.
Keep detailed scoring formulas and edge cases in **Full rules**, and Journey
accuracy thresholds in **Accuracy**. Keep necessary gameplay restrictions visible.
Translate internal terms such as “distractor pool” and “bundled catalog” into
ordinary language. Keep technical source specifications in Sources or credits.

## Feedback, reveals, and recovery

Keep feedback brief and proportional: “Well spotted.”, “Nicely placed.”, or
“Not quite.” Name the answer naturally in the explanation, then explain its
geographic significance. Preserve additional useful context and the Journey’s
voice. The shared answer line and map must still show the solution clearly,
including cases with several valid answers or placements.

Describe recoverable errors with what happened, what is affected or preserved,
and an available next action. Prefer “Couldn’t” to “Could not.” Claim progress is
saved only when the relevant save has succeeded; an open in-memory session is
not proof of persistence. Keep local-save and cloud-sync failures distinct.
Do not advertise forthcoming features unless that promise is intentional.

Match empty states and remedies to actual behavior. Study history includes
sessions ended early. Pinpoint’s empty pool may require enabling categories.
Globe requirements must not obscure working written-answer fallbacks.
