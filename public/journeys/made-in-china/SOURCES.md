# Made in China — sources and media

## Geography and factual scope — revision 2

Reviewed 2026-10-26. Each question retains its institutional sources in the player’s Sources panel.

- **Food:** [FAO’s regional staple-food account](https://www.fao.org/4/J0962e/J0962e00.htm) supports the broad northern wheat / southern rice inference. This is a food tradition, not an exclusive crop map. The [Xi’an local standard](https://std.samr.gov.cn/db/search/stdDBDetailed?id=0DA2AA7B7EC733EEE06397BE0A0A8797) supports the biangbiang noodle association. Chengdu and Guangzhou are comparison cities.
- **Porcelain:** [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1765/), the [Smithsonian’s ceramics account](https://publications.asia.si.edu/seaceramics/place/location.php?group=7&key=102), its [kiln-fuel account](https://festival.si.edu/2014/china/from-the-land/porcelain/smithsonian), and [UNESCO’s industry landscape evaluation](https://whc.unesco.org/document/226090) support local kaolin/porcelain stone, wood fuel and river transport. The local Chang River must not be confused with the Yangtze, also called Chang Jiang. No invented mineral-deposit boundaries or historical shipping route is drawn.
- **Fort:** [UNESCO’s Jiayuguan account](https://whc.unesco.org/document/162535) locates the fort between the Heishan and Qilian mountains. Site B approximates the fort at 39.802° N, 98.217° E; A and C are authored comparison sites in the northern and southern mountain terrain, not historical forts. All three use equal markers. The grading contract selects one site; it never grades pixels in the terrain image.
- **Terrain:** NASA Earth Observatory / GIBS Blue Marble Shaded Relief Bathymetry, geographic WMS crop [96.4, 38.8, 100.0, 40.7], 1840 × 1280. The unmodified JPEG, download URL and checksum are recorded in `src/game/data/made-in-china-context.v1.json`. [NASA media terms](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) apply. This is modern explanatory terrain, not a reconstruction of an ancient landscape, road or wall. It appears only during the fort question and reveal.
- **Trading gateway:** [Shanghai’s geographic overview](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-Overview/20231209/705180b43f794c1ca43f4d1ddaa049a2.html) supports its Yangtze/sea setting; [Shanghai’s Customs House account](https://touch.shio.gov.cn/jsp/zfxw_detail.jsp?id=20250929181837586) dates the specific building to 1927. Nanjing is an upriver comparator and Ningbo a coastal comparator south of the estuary. This is not a claim of a single Jingdezhen-to-Shanghai export route. A second NASA Blue Marble crop shows the actual modern river and estuary together, avoiding a gap in the bundled generalized river line. Its geographic bounds [114.0, 27.0, 127.5, 36.0], unchanged 1600 × 1280 WMS download, attribution and checksum accompany the fort crop in the context JSON. The image is a geographic clue, not a grading surface or a 1927 reconstruction.
- **Shenzhen:** The [World Bank’s special economic zone study](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/310891468018256346/pdf/WPS5583.pdf) supports Hong Kong’s role in investment and business connections. [Shenzhen’s government](https://www.sz.gov.cn/en_szgov/news/latest/content/post_8174716.html) dates the zone to 1980. The task compares Shenzhen with Dongguan and Huizhou, rather than requiring a precision pin. Names remain withheld until reveal; Hong Kong is a reference throughout.
- City coordinates reuse the committed GeoNames snapshot. Hong Kong is GeoNames 1819729 (22.27832° N, 114.17469° E). Jiangxi’s geometry is copied unchanged from `public/geography/chn-adm1.v1.json` (SHA-256 `21eeac8c0ae7ce4da945ed505350455335132f9d76744a75026403930d830dfd`). No shared geography was regenerated.

The older photo and province subsets remain as source assets from revision 1; they are not all displayed in revision 2. No province-membership, landmark-ordering or distance-band claim remains in the current questions.

The dim sum photograph documents a Hong Kong breakfast as an illustration of the Cantonese tradition; it is not represented as photographed in Guangzhou. The porcelain vessel is a Yuan-dynasty Jingdezhen object photographed at the Shanghai Museum. The kiln photograph shows a kiln at Jingdezhen and does not claim that this exact structure dates to a particular dynasty.

The archival Shenzhen photograph is described by its photographer as taken in **1982**; the Commons upload/metadata date of 2014 is not the historical scene date. The contemporary skyline was photographed in **2025** from Lianhuashan Park and shows the Civic Centre and Futian’s dense business district. The comparison uses different viewpoints and is not presented as a precisely aligned repeat photograph.
